They also offer various panels and attack statistics to provide victim management capabilities to their affiliates.   </p> <p>The malware uses the double extortion technique to compel victims into paying ransoms. Through this technique, <a href="https://cyble.com/blog/data-protection-an-integral-part-of-cyber-defence/" title="Data Protection an Integral Part of Cyber Defence" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-wpil-monitor-id="11332">attackers exfiltrate the victim’s</a> data, after which they proceed to encrypt the data on the victim’s system. Data encryption is followed by the TAs demand ransom in exchange for a decryptor. If the victim refuses or cannot pay the ransom, the TA threatens to <a href="https://cyble.com/blog/sensitive-details-of-over-200-million-indonesian-residents-allegedly-leaked/" title="Sensitive Details of Over 200 Million Indonesian Residents Allegedly Leaked" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-wpil-monitor-id="11336">leak</a> the data. This <a class="wpil_keyword_link" href="https://cyble.com/knowledge-hub/what-is-ransomware/" title="ransomware" data-wpil-keyword-link="linked" data-wpil-monitor-id="1047" target="_blank">ransomware</a> was previously known as ABCD ransomware as the file extension used for encrypting files was .abcd. Now the extension used by this ransomware is <em>.lockbit</em>.   </p> <p>Figure 1 shows the LOCKBIT 2.0 ransomware gang hosting a blog in the TOR network. This blog, in particular, is used by the TA to share the list of victims and screenshots of the sample <a
